\section*{Signed Interpolation for Partial Relational Tasks}

This walkthrough explains why the signed interpolation $(1-\gamma)X - \gamma I$ enables dual decoders to solve both feature and position tasks simultaneously, while standard interpolation $(1-\gamma)X + \gamma I$ fails.

\subsection*{The Problem: Entangled Information}

For a task requiring both feature and position prediction, we need the attention output to preserve both types of information. Consider the standard adaptable bottleneck:

\[
V = \bigl[(1-\gamma)X_c + \gamma I\bigr]W_v
\]

At intermediate $\gamma$ values (e.g., $\gamma = 0.5$), the value matrix becomes:
\[
V = 0.5 X_c + 0.5 I
\]

\textbf{Example} ($L=3$): Context item at position 1 with feature 2:
\[
X_c[\text{pos 1}] = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
\]

After value interpolation ($\gamma=0.5$):
\[
V[\text{pos 1}] = 0.5 \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} + 0.5 \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix}
\]

\textbf{Problem}: Feature info (index 2) and position info (index 0) are now \emph{mixed} in the same vector. Linear decoders cannot separate them:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Feature decoder $W_f$ needs to extract ``feature = 2'' from $[0.5, 0, 0.5]$
    \item Position decoder $W_p$ needs to extract ``position = 0'' from the \emph{same} vector $[0.5, 0, 0.5]$
\end{itemize}

There exists no linear projection that can simultaneously recover both pieces of information from an entangled representation.

\subsection*{The Solution: Signed Interpolation}

Change the sign on the identity term:
\[
V = \bigl[(1-\gamma)X_c - \gamma I\bigr]W_v
\]

At $\gamma = 0.5$:
\[
V = 0.5 X_c - 0.5 I
\]

\textbf{Same example}: Position 1, feature 2:
\[
V[\text{pos 1}] = 0.5 \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} - 0.5 \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} -0.5 & 0 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix}
\]

\textbf{Key insight}: The opposite signs create \emph{separable} components:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Positive component} ($+0.5$ at index 2): feature information
    \item \textbf{Negative component} ($-0.5$ at index 0): position information
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{How Dual Decoders Extract Information}

The dual decoders learn to separate the signed components:

\subsubsection*{Feature Decoder Strategy}

$W_f$ learns to pick out \emph{positive} activations (feature signal from $X_c$):
\[
W_f \approx \begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 1 \\
\end{bmatrix}
\quad \text{(identity-like, captures positive part)}
\]

\[
\text{logits}_f = V \cdot W_f = \begin{bmatrix} -0.5 & 0 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 1 \\
\end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} -0.5 & 0 & \mathbf{0.5} \end{bmatrix}
\]

$\arg\max = 2$ \checkmark \quad (correct feature)

\subsubsection*{Position Decoder Strategy}

$W_p$ learns to pick out \emph{negative} activations (position signal from $-I$):
\[
W_p \approx \begin{bmatrix}
-1 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & -1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & -1 \\
\end{bmatrix}
\quad \text{(negative identity, flips sign)}
\]

\[
\text{logits}_p = V \cdot W_p = \begin{bmatrix} -0.5 & 0 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
-1 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & -1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & -1 \\
\end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{0.5} & 0 & -0.5 \end{bmatrix}
\]

$\arg\max = 0$ \checkmark \quad (correct position)

\subsection*{General Principle: Differential Encoding}

The signed interpolation creates a \textbf{differential encoding}:
\[
V[\text{pos } i, \text{ feature } j] = (1-\gamma) \cdot e_j - \gamma \cdot e_i
\]

where $e_k$ is the $k$-th basis vector. This is analogous to:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Signal processing}: In-phase vs. quadrature components (I/Q modulation)
    \item \textbf{Neuroscience}: ON vs. OFF cells in retinal encoding
    \item \textbf{Linear algebra}: Encoding information in orthogonal subspaces
\end{itemize}

The opposite polarities act as \emph{tags} that make the information linearly separable, enabling simple linear decoders to extract both signals from the same vector.

\hrule
\vspace{1em}

\textbf{Conclusion}: Standard interpolation $(1-\gamma)X + \gamma I$ entangles feature and position information at intermediate $\gamma$ values. The signed variant $(1-\gamma)X - \gamma I$ uses opposite signs to create separable components that dual linear decoders can extract independently.
